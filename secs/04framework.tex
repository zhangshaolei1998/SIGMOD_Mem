\section{Query Planning for Accuracy}
\label{sec:framework}

A memory query determines which facts should be combined and which
dependencies should be resolved. The current memory state determines
how much of that work is already complete. We use these two inputs
together: a query-specific pipeline specifies how to obtain the required
facts and combine them into a result;
first-order facts supply reusable values; second-order experience guides
their access and the cost of unresolved work. Optimization acts on the
execution of this computation: which evidence to read, which subresults
to reuse, and which acquisition or verification action to perform next.
Section~\ref{sec:executor} gives the selection objective and stopping rule.
Semantic operators and subtask graphs provide related
abstractions~\cite{lotus,docetl,decomposed,lmcompiler}.

\subsection{Query Plans and Evidence Requirements}
\label{subsec:new-plans}

The three plans are \emph{single-fact retrieval} (\ret),
\emph{independent composition} (\red), and
\emph{dependent composition} (\comp).
The first is the base case. The other two distinguish whether one
evidence request needs a value produced by another.
Independent composition includes counts, comparisons, summaries, and
tuples of unrelated facts; it is not restricted to numeric aggregation.
Dependent composition includes answer-dependent entity or time bindings,
with independent branches allowed inside the plan.

The compiler produces $P=(R,D,g)$ for $q$.
$R=(r_1,\ldots,r_d)$, $d\geq1$, contains bounded lookup or population-block
request templates. $g$ is a type-checked result operation, and
$(j,i,p)\in D$ binds the output of $r_j$ to input slot $p$ of $r_i$.
Each template carries $q$'s source snapshot and declared scopes.
Slots accept scalar or finite-set values and have at most one binding.

Normalization exposes cross-goal bindings and removes unused requests
and identity-only wrappers. Candidate enumeration, independent evaluation, and reduction
form one population block; a result that determines a later request
creates an explicit edge. The graph is finite and acyclic.

A plan is \emph{atomic} when it requests one individual fact and $g$
projects its value. Population operations remain non-atomic even with
zero or one observed candidate. Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:planner}
\rho(P)=
\begin{cases}
\ret, &D=\varnothing,\ P\text{ atomic},\\
\red, &D=\varnothing,\ P\text{ non-atomic},\\
\comp,&D\neq\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

The partition is complete and disjoint: a plan either has a binding
edge or has none; an edge-free plan is either atomic or non-atomic.
Each well-formed normalized finite acyclic plan therefore selects
exactly one pipeline.

This classification fixes the computation's coverage and dependency
requirements. Evidence sources, access order, reuse, and execution
budget remain state-dependent decisions. Across queries, a new session can enlarge a
population; a verified fact can eliminate acquisition; a resolved
predecessor can unlock a lookup. Query processing adapts these decisions
while retaining the plan's evidence requirements.

The compiler supplies one normalized plan and validates its entity/time
scopes, bindings, and $g$. The partition applies to that plan; different
semantics-preserving representations of a question can expose different
bindings. For example, a longest-activity query can request the winner's
organizer after comparison or acquire every organizer before selection.
Once a plan is fixed, evidence access, reuse, action ordering, and
stopping are selected from the current memory state. Model-assisted
compilation is charged; ambiguous scope leaves a required check unresolved.

\subsection{Experience-Guided Evidence Access}
\label{subsec:new-experience}

\emph{Grounding} obtains source-backed evidence for a request.
All pipelines use $E_i=\operatorname{Ground}(r_i,\Mm)$ against $q$'s
fixed source snapshot. Grounding checks identity, scope, source, and
version, reuses eligible facts, and acquires missing evidence from raw
sessions (Section~\ref{subsec:new-evolution}). Experience orders facts
and source regions without replacing these checks. Paid acquisition and
semantic verification are bounded actions (Section~\ref{sec:executor}).

For each request, grounding first checks reusable fact versions and
their required supports. A valid cache hit discharges that evidence
work; a missing value, changed scope, or unresolved version leaves work
for acquisition or verification. The priority in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pi}
orders eligible alternatives within this residual work. Population
coverage and predecessor checks remain required throughout execution.
This separates opportunities to reduce evidence work from the checks
that establish the result.

\runin{Single-fact retrieval}
For an individual target, ground its evidence and select the
source-supported value at the requested time:
$v=\operatorname{Select}(r,E)$.
Unresolved identity, conflicting versions, or absent support leaves a
gap; a missing fact remains unknown.
Verified cached evidence can be returned without another semantic judgment.

\runin{Independent composition}
With no cross-request bindings, ground the requests independently and
apply $g$ to their values. A tuple can combine a preference and an allergy.
For a population block, establish its scope, deduplicate identities,
then obtain each candidate's evidence. For the current population request $r$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:new-reduce}
\begin{aligned}
x_e&=\operatorname{Eval}(r,e,E_e),\\
v&=\operatorname{Combine}(r,\{(e,x_e):e\in\operatorname{scope}(r)\}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$x_e$ is a verified value or predicate outcome, or $\bot$ if unresolved.
Counts use yes/no judgments; comparisons align normalized values with
their candidates; summaries retain source support.
$\operatorname{Combine}$ implements the request's count, comparison,
tuple, or source-grounded synthesis; $g$ combines request outputs.

Independent judgments may execute in parallel. A closed empty scope has
a defined result, such as count 0 or no matching event.
Comparisons retain all tied winners. When a later lookup consumes a winner,
that binding makes the containing plan dependent and instantiates one
request per bound winner, preserving ties.

An exact population result needs complete coverage and every required
judgment. With $h$ known positives and $u$ unresolved judgments in a
closed scope, return $[h,h+u]$ with partial support.
Without coverage, only $h$ is a justified lower bound.
A highly ranked subset cannot establish an exact count.

\runin{Dependent composition}
A node is ready only when every required predecessor has
$\sigma_j=\texttt{supported}$. Evaluate ready nodes in topological
order, then finalize $g$ only when its required inputs are supported:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:new-compose}
\begin{aligned}
r_i^*&=r_i[p\leftarrow v_j:(j,i,p)\in D],\\
m_i&=(v_i,E_i,\sigma_i)=\operatorname{Solve}(r_i^*,\Mm),\\
v&=g(v_1,\ldots,v_d).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$\operatorname{Solve}$ executes the normalized retrieval or independent
block. Ready branches can run concurrently; a bound request returns
to grounding for its newly determined target.
A blocked node retains its unresolved predecessor check and waits
before being passed to $\operatorname{Solve}$.

Available supports yield a partial response; no established evidence
yields unknown. A definitive final value is computed only from
the inputs required by $g$.
A supported predecessor with no matching target instead propagates
supported absence and skips the target lookup, as specified by $g$;
the binding is then resolved as absence.

\runin{Support invariant}
The final $m=(v,E,\sigma)$ is supported only if its plan is validated and
its required identity, source, version, coverage, value, binding, and
result checks are discharged; $E$ carries their
supports, including the dependency ancestors of projected outputs and
their selection and exclusion evidence.
Otherwise the result remains partial or unknown.
With correct primitive checks and scope interpretation, independent
reduction and dependency-ordered binding preserve this invariant by
induction. Use scores and budgets cannot close unresolved checks.

\begin{memoryexample}{Example 2: evidence requirements of the three plans}
Use Alice's three March activities from Example~1.

\smallskip
\textbf{Lookup.} \emph{Who organized the hike?}
Verify \texttt{hike-1}'s organizer and return Ava.

\smallskip
\textbf{Independent.} \emph{How many March activities were outdoors?}
Evaluate hike $\to$ yes, pottery $\to$ no, ride $\to$ yes:
$1+0+1=2$. If pottery's setting were unknown, return $[2,3]$.

\smallskip
\textbf{Dependent.} \emph{Who organized my longest outdoor activity in March?}
Acquire durations: hike, 4 hours; ride, 3 hours.
Select \texttt{hike-1}, bind it to the organizer lookup, and return Ava.
An unresolved winner cannot justify choosing Ava over Cara.

\smallskip
Retain new durations in $\Ff$. Reported result use credits its
supporting facts, including exclusion evidence.
\end{memoryexample}
