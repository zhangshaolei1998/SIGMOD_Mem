\section{For Performance: Experience-Guided Memory Access}
\label{sec:framework}

A memory query needs evidence organized into its required computation,
not just relevant passages. The pipelines distinguish a fact lookup from
independent and dependent composition. Second-order experience guides
grounding without changing support requirements.

\subsection{Fact Lookup and Evidence Composition}
\label{subsec:new-plans}

The three plans are \emph{single-fact retrieval} (\ret),
\emph{independent composition} (\red), and
\emph{dependent composition} (\comp).
The first is the base case. The other two distinguish whether one
evidence request needs a value produced by another.
Independent composition includes counts, comparisons, summaries, and
tuples of unrelated facts; it is not restricted to numeric aggregation.
Dependent composition includes answer-dependent entity or time bindings,
with independent branches allowed inside the plan.

The compiler produces $P=(R,D,g)$ for $q$.
$R=(r_1,\ldots,r_d)$, $d\geq1$, contains bounded lookup or population-block
request templates. $g$ is a type-checked result operation, and
$(j,i,p)\in D$ binds the output of $r_j$ to input slot $p$ of $r_i$.
Each template carries $q$'s source snapshot and declared scopes.
Slots accept scalar or finite-set values and have at most one binding.
Normalization exposes cross-goal bindings and removes unused requests
and identity-only wrappers. Candidate enumeration, independent evaluation, and reduction
form one population block; a result that determines a later request
creates an explicit edge. The graph is finite and acyclic.

A plan is \emph{atomic} when it requests one individual fact and $g$
projects its value. Population operations remain non-atomic even with
zero or one observed candidate. Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:planner}
\rho(P)=
\begin{cases}
\ret, &D=\varnothing,\ P\text{ atomic},\\
\red, &D=\varnothing,\ P\text{ non-atomic},\\
\comp,&D\neq\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Complete and disjoint plan classes]
Every well-formed normalized finite acyclic evidence plan belongs to exactly one
class in Eq.~\eqref{eq:planner}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A plan either has a dependency edge or has none. In the latter case
it is either atomic or non-atomic. The cases are exhaustive and
mutually exclusive.
\end{proof}

This is a guarantee about plans, not intrinsic labels for natural-language
questions. For the longest-outdoor-activity organizer, we use a goal-directed
plan: select the event, then bind it to an organizer lookup.
An alternative that fetches every organizer before selecting could be
independent. Both plans represent the same question, but incur different
work. The compiler exposes the chosen computation and validates entity/time
scopes, bindings, and $g$. Model-assisted compilation is charged when
needed; ambiguous scope is clarified or reported as a gap.
The partition does not prove that compilation always interprets a
question correctly.

\subsection{Learning to Ground and Execute}
\label{subsec:new-experience}

\input{secs/05secondorder}

In Example~1, with illustrative priors $\alpha_0=\beta_0=1$, the Ava/Cara organizer
links have estimated use propensity $2/3$ and Ben's has $1/3$ in a
matched contact context.
This changes the priority of equally relevant facts, not their truth;
an indoor-activity request can still favor Ben.

All pipelines use $E_i=\operatorname{Ground}(r_i,\Mm)$ against $q$'s
fixed source snapshot.
Grounding checks identity, scope, source, and version, reuses eligible
facts, and acquires unmet evidence from raw sessions
(Section~\ref{subsec:new-evolution}). Experience orders facts and source
regions without replacing those checks.

\runin{Single-fact retrieval: locate, verify, return}
For an individual target, select its source-supported temporal value
$v=\operatorname{Select}(r,E)$.
Missing facts are not automatically false; unresolved identity,
conflicting versions, or absent support yields a gap.
Verified cached evidence can be returned without another semantic judgment.

\runin{Independent composition: ground, evaluate, combine}
With no cross-request bindings, ground the requests independently and
apply $g$ to their values. A tuple can combine a preference and an allergy.
For a population block, establish its scope, deduplicate identities,
then obtain each candidate's evidence. For the current population request $r$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:new-reduce}
\begin{aligned}
x_e&=\operatorname{Eval}(r,e,E_e),\\
v&=\operatorname{Combine}(r,\{(e,x_e):e\in\operatorname{scope}(r)\}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$x_e$ is a verified value or predicate outcome, or $\bot$ if unresolved.
Counts use yes/no judgments; comparisons align normalized values with
their candidates; summaries retain source support.
$\operatorname{Combine}$ implements the request's count, comparison,
tuple, or source-grounded synthesis; $g$ combines request outputs.
Independent judgments may execute in parallel. A closed empty scope has a defined result, such as count
0 or no matching event, rather than an unknown value.
Comparisons retain all tied winners; a later lookup instantiates one
request per bound winner rather than silently choosing one.

An exact population result needs complete coverage and every required
judgment. With $h$ known positives and $u$ unresolved judgments in a
closed scope, return $[h,h+u]$ with partial support.
Without coverage, only $h$ is a justified lower bound.
A highly ranked subset cannot establish an exact count.

\runin{Dependent composition: resolve, bind, continue}
A node is ready only when every required predecessor has
$\sigma_j=\texttt{supported}$. Evaluate ready nodes in topological
order, then finalize $g$ only when its required inputs are supported:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:new-compose}
\begin{aligned}
r_i^*&=r_i[p\leftarrow v_j:(j,i,p)\in D],\\
m_i&=(v_i,E_i,\sigma_i)=\operatorname{Solve}(r_i^*,\Mm),\\
v&=g(v_1,\ldots,v_d).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$\operatorname{Solve}$ executes the normalized retrieval or independent
block. Ready branches can run concurrently; a bound request returns
to grounding for its newly determined target.
A blocked node is not passed to $\operatorname{Solve}$:
it retains the unresolved predecessor obligation.
Available supports yield a partial response; no established evidence
yields unknown. A definitive final value is computed only from
the inputs required by $g$.
A supported predecessor with no matching target instead propagates
supported absence and skips the target lookup, as specified by $g$;
it is not an unresolved binding.

\runin{Support invariant}
The final $m=(v,E,\sigma)$ is supported only if its plan is validated and
its required identity, source, version, coverage, value, binding, and
result checks are discharged; $E$ carries their
supports, including the dependency ancestors of projected outputs and
their selection and exclusion evidence.
Otherwise the result remains partial or unknown.
Conditional on correct primitive checks and scope interpretation,
this rule is preserved by independent reduction and, inductively, by
dependency-ordered binding. Neither a high use score nor a larger budget
can certify an unresolved obligation. This is a support guarantee
relative to the validated plan, not a guarantee of error-free LLM extraction.

\begin{memoryexample}{Example 2: one fact, independent evidence, dependent evidence}
Use Alice's three March activities from Example~1.

\smallskip
\textbf{Lookup.} \emph{Who organized the hike?}
Verify \texttt{hike-1}'s organizer and return Ava.

\smallskip
\textbf{Independent.} \emph{How many activities were outdoors?}
Evaluate hike $\to$ yes, pottery $\to$ no, ride $\to$ yes:
$1+0+1=2$. If pottery's setting were unknown, return $[2,3]$.

\smallskip
\textbf{Dependent.} \emph{Who organized the longest outdoor activity?}
Acquire durations: hike, 4 hours; ride, 3 hours.
Select \texttt{hike-1}, bind it to the organizer lookup, and return Ava.
An unresolved winner cannot justify choosing Ava over Cara.

\smallskip
Retain new durations in $\Ff$. A consumed-result use report credits its
supporting facts, including exclusion evidence.
\end{memoryexample}
