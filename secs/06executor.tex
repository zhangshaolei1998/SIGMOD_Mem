\section{Cost-Aware Execution}
\label{sec:executor}

A memory system that answers well but spends unboundedly does not scale to long histories and heavy usage. To control this cost while preserving accuracy, \sys{} introduces a cost-aware executor that exploits the query patterns identified by the planner.

The reason is that different query patterns have different cost profiles. A simple fact lookup (\point{}) retrieves one stored value and never benefits from repeated sampling, while an aggregation (\agg{}) or comparison (\cmp{}) over multiple records genuinely reduces variance with additional samples. Treating all queries the same, as uniform self-consistency does, wastes budget on easy queries and cannot reallocate it to hard ones.

The executor addresses this through two complementary mechanisms: it allocates different budgets to different query patterns (Section~\ref{subsec:budget}), and it stops spending early when an individual query's answer is already stable (Section~\ref{subsec:shortcircuit}). The two act on different dimensions and their savings compose.

\stitle{Cost Model.}
\sys{} issues LLM calls on both the write path (catalog extraction) and the read path (query answering). Write-path cost is paid once per session and amortizes across queries. The executor targets read-path cost, which dominates. Under fixed per-pattern budgets $(K_{\point},K_{\agg},K_{\cmp})$, the total cost over a workload of $Q$ queries with pattern fractions $(p_{\point},p_{\agg},p_{\cmp})$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cost}
C \;=\; Q\cdot\bigl(p_{\point}K_{\point} + p_{\agg}K_{\agg} + p_{\cmp}K_{\cmp}\bigr).
\end{equation}
A Uniform-$K{=}3$ reference sets $K_{\point}=K_{\agg}=K_{\cmp}=3$, so $C=3Q$.

\subsection{Pattern-Aware Budgeting}
\label{subsec:budget}

To avoid wasting LLM calls on queries that do not benefit from replication, the executor assigns a budget based on the query pattern, giving each pattern the budget its operator can use productively.

Specifically, the executor sets $K_{\point}=1$ and $K_{\agg}=K_{\cmp}=3$. The asymmetry follows from what each operator computes. A \point{} query resolves a single stored fact: once $\textsf{(Alice, has-provider, Raiffeisen Basel)}$ is in the context, re-sampling can only re-read the same value, and one call is both necessary and sufficient. \agg{} and \cmp{} queries compose evidence across multiple records, as when the outdoor count must decide eight independent \texttt{yes}/\texttt{no} judgments, and there sampling genuinely reduces variance, so these patterns retain the full budget.

Substituting these budgets into Eq.~(\ref{eq:cost}), the saving against Uniform-$K{=}3$ is $\Delta C=2p_{\point}Q$. On \membench{} with $p_{\point}=0.61$, this alone reduces $C$ from $900$ to $532$, a $40.9\%$ cut. The saving grows with the share of point queries and costs nothing in accuracy on the queries it economizes.

\subsection{Agreement-Based Early Stopping}
\label{subsec:shortcircuit}

To further reduce cost on the harder query patterns, the executor detects when additional sampling is unnecessary for a specific query. Some \agg{} and \cmp{} instances are easy but indistinguishable from hard ones before execution. The executor recovers this distinction at no extra cost by reading the agreement between samples it was going to draw anyway.

Specifically, within the \agg/\cmp{} budget, the executor draws two independent samples $p_1,p_2$ and measures their token F1 $\mathrm{agree}(\cdot,\cdot)\in[0,1]$. If the two samples agree above a threshold $\theta$, the query is considered settled and no third sample is drawn:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:short}
\hat{a} \;=\; \begin{cases}
p_1, & \mathrm{agree}(p_1,p_2)\geq\theta, \\
\mathrm{Majority}(p_1,p_2,p_3), & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
On Alice's outdoor query, both samples enumerate the same eight events and emit \texttt{Answer: 3}, so the agreement is high, the third call is skipped, and the query costs two calls, saving the third. Had one sample returned \texttt{3} and the other \texttt{4}, the low agreement would have triggered the third call, spending it where it can change the outcome. We fix $\theta=0.6$ for all benchmarks and all backbones.

Combined with pattern-aware budgeting, early stopping drives the total cost on \membench{} from $532$ to $444$, a further $16.5\%$ cut and $50.7\%$ overall against the Uniform-$K{=}3$ reference.

\stitle{Remarks.}
Self-consistency~\cite{sc} treats disagreement among samples as noise to average away under a fixed budget. The same disagreement, however, also carries information about the query: when two samples agree, the query is easy for the current model and grounding, and a third sample is unlikely to change the outcome. Agreement thus becomes a stopping rule. Adaptive-Consistency~\cite{adaptiveconsistency} and early-stopping self-consistency~\cite{escsc} exploit the same observation within a single query, halting once drawn answers converge, but they must draw at least two samples before any decision is possible. Because \sys{} knows the query pattern, it can go further: a point lookup cannot benefit from a second sample at all, so the extra sample is skipped entirely for the majority of the workload. The two mechanisms, pattern-aware budgeting across patterns and agreement-based stopping within a pattern, therefore save from disjoint slices and their savings compose.
