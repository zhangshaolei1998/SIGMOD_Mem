\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate memory organization with source-linked agent--memory experience and
workload-aware queries for accuracy and cost. We examine overall
accuracy, component contributions, adaptation across queries, and
history-length scaling, followed by an end-to-end example.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\input{tables/datasets}

\stitle{Datasets.}
Table~\ref{tbl:benchmarks} groups eight benchmarks by memory ability.
\locomo{} tests multi-session dialogue~\cite{locomo}; \lmev{} tests
environment experience from web-agent trajectories~\cite{longmemevalv2}.
\lmeo{}, \lmes{}, and \lmem{} vary evidence availability and history
length~\cite{longmemeval}. \evomem{} tests adaptation across task
streams~\cite{evomemory}; \dynamicmem{} tracks evolving user
profiles~\cite{dynamicmem}.
We use \membench{}~\cite{membench} to study single-fact retrieval,
independent composition, dependent composition, and cost-aware
execution. Together, these benchmarks evaluate the memory hierarchy and its
accumulation of user-specific evidence and second-order agent--memory
experience over time.

\stitle{Baselines.}
The seven baselines are Full-Context,
RAG~\cite{rag}, MemGPT~\cite{memgpt}, Mem0 and
Mem0$^{g}$~\cite{mem0}, Zep~\cite{zep}, and
LongMemEval-Fw~\cite{longmemeval}. They provide, respectively,
full-history prompting, passage retrieval, context tiers,
consolidated facts and entity relations, temporal graphs, and an
indexing--retrieval--reading pipeline.
We compare methods under a shared backbone and complement the overall
comparison with component ablations.

\stitle{Metrics and Settings.}
Multiple-choice tasks use exact match; open-answer tasks use F1 with
benchmark-specific normalization. Comparisons are within a benchmark's
metric scale.
The default backbone is GPT-5.5; Section~\ref{subsec:exp2} covers four closed-source and
four open-source models. Every reported call count uses $C(q)$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost}; averages report mean $C(q)$, and totals report
$\sum_q C(q)$.

\subsection{Overall Accuracy}
\label{subsec:exp1}

Table~\ref{tbl:main_results} shows that \sys{} matches or improves the
strongest evaluated baseline in every column. The mean benchmark-score
margin is $2.6$ points.
The gains vary with the memory ability being tested.

\stitle{Accuracy over long histories.}
The margins on \lmeo{}, \lmes{}, and \lmem{} are $5.0$, $5.6$, and
$4.9$ points. A query over many sessions has to recover the right
entities and temporal versions before computing its answer.
The architecture in Section~\ref{sec:overview} separates this source
history from reusable fact values; the pipelines in
Section~\ref{sec:framework} then expose coverage and binding requirements.

The result is consistent with reducing the evidence alignment left to
a long generation, a difficulty observed in long-context
evaluations~\cite{longcontext,ruler}. Component ablations in
Section~\ref{subsec:exp5} separately test reusable facts and explicit
composition.

\stitle{Lookup and composition workloads.}
The margins are $0.6$ points on \locomo{} and $1.6$ on \membench{}.
Sections~\ref{subsec:exp3} and~\ref{subsec:exp7routing} examine the
evidence processing and plan compilation behind these aggregate scores.

\stitle{Changing user state.}
On \evomem{}, \sys{} gains $2.5$ points over the strongest baseline;
on \dynamicmem{}, both systems score $34.9$.
Section~\ref{subsec:exp6} examines query positions on \evomem{};
Section~\ref{subsec:exp5} tests second-order experience with unchanged
aggregate $\sum_q C(q)$.

\subsection{Evidence Requirements and Query Accuracy}
\label{subsec:exp3}

The three pipelines differ in the evidence checks they require.
Single-fact retrieval terminates once the requested value and its support
are established. For population queries, independent composition also requires
coverage and a supported judgment for each candidate. Dependent composition
preserves the event--value bindings needed by a later selection or lookup.

\stitle{Independent composition requires population coverage.}
A count can be wrong even when the retrieved passages are topically
relevant: candidates can be omitted or duplicated, and a mentioned
event may not satisfy the requested predicate.
The independent-composition pipeline in Eq.~\eqref{eq:new-reduce} separates
enumeration, per-candidate judgment, and tallying.
In Alice's example, establishing that pottery was indoors justifies its
exclusion from the outdoor count. Once each judgment is supported,
arithmetic need not be another unstructured model inference.

\stitle{Dependent composition preserves value--event bindings.}
A dependent composition should associate each normalized value with its event
before ordering them. Retrieving both durations without that alignment
can still select the wrong activity. When the selected activity determines
an organizer request, Eq.~\eqref{eq:new-compose} carries the required
binding into the next lookup.

\subsection{Cost-Aware Execution}
\label{subsec:exp4}

Table~\ref{tbl:cost} separates two sources of savings in cost-aware
execution: allocating effort across queries and selectively refining
evidence only while it remains worthwhile.

\stitle{Query-specific allocation.}
Fixed Budget ($K{=}3$) has $\sum_q C(q)=900$ at $89.1$ accuracy.
The allocation-only configuration has $\sum_q C(q)=532$ at $92.8$:
$368$ lower $\sum_q C(q)$ ($40.9\%$) with a higher score.
These aggregate results favor query-specific allocation over a uniform
call allowance. In the method, the reserved work depends on unresolved
checks and the historical benefit and cost of available actions.

\stitle{Selective refinement.}
Selective refinement reduces $\sum_q C(q)$ from $532$ to $444$, saving another $88$
($16.5\%$ of the allocation-only count), with the same reported aggregate
accuracy. Average $C(q)$ is $1.00$ for single-fact retrieval, $2.30$ for
independent composition, and $2.18$ for dependent composition.

The realized costs fall below their allocated maxima. In total,
$\sum_q C(q)$ falls by $456$ relative to Fixed Budget ($K{=}3$),
giving the reported $50.7\%$ reduction.
The allocation and refinement configurations evaluate the two controls
together with the pipeline. Allocation reserves admissible work;
refinement releases reservations and rebuilds the selected work after
each outcome. The additional $88$ saved calls quantify refinement's
contribution at the same aggregate score. The reported plan-class means
summarize realized cost; action selection follows the current evidence
state through Eq.~\eqref{eq:short}.

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} summarizes the accuracy--cost tradeoff under $C(q)$.

\subsection{Component Ablations}
\label{subsec:exp5}

Table~\ref{tbl:ablation} compares configurations of the memory
hierarchy, query-specific pipelines, second-order agent--memory experience,
and cost-aware execution.

\stitle{Memory organization and query computation.}
Removing first-order facts lowers accuracy by $6.1$ points on
\membench{} and $2.7$ on \lmev{}.
Removing composition pipelines lowers it by $4.9$ and $2.0$; removing query-plan classification
lowers it by $3.7$ and $1.5$.
Each configuration also has a larger $\sum_q C(q)$ than the full system.

These losses occur despite greater aggregate call cost. First-order
facts supply reusable operands; the selected pipeline makes their
composition explicit. Removing either increases repeated grounding or
leaves evidence combination to generation. Raw-session grounding also
preserves access to details that no earlier query materialized.
\stitle{Second-order experience at unchanged aggregate cost.}
Removing second-order experience loses $0.7$ points on \membench{} and
$1.7$ on \lmev{}, while $\sum_q C(q)$ remains $444$ and $602$.
Removing experience-guided priority loses $0.3$ and $1.0$, again without changing
those totals. The whole-experience ablation removes the history used by
both access priorities and action estimates; the priority ablation
targets the access score in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pi}.

The score differences associate interaction experience with better
answers at unchanged aggregate expenditure. The two ablations test
different uses of that state: evidence ordering and the combined
access/execution policy.

\stitle{Allocation and refinement.}
Without cost-aware allocation, $\sum_q C(q)$ increases by $312$
on \membench{} and $301$ on \lmev{}; accuracy falls by $0.3$ on the
former and is unchanged on the latter.
Without selective refinement, $\sum_q C(q)$ increases by $88$ and $121$
without changing the reported scores. Allocation affects both quality and
cost; selective refinement removes work after allocation.

\input{tables/routing}

The LLM compiler improves answer accuracy by $0.4$ points.

\subsection{Scaling with History Length}
\label{subsec:exp8}

\input{tables/scaling}

Table~\ref{tbl:scaling} increases history from $40$K to $1.5$M tokens,
a $37.5\times$ range. Average $C(q)$ remains $1.9$ for \sys{}
and $1.0$ for Full-Context. Yet latency grows $20.7\times$ for
Full-Context, from $2.3$s to $47.6$s, and only $1.09\times$ for
\sys{}, from $3.2$s to $3.5$s. $C(q)$ alone therefore understates
the differences in inference expense.

At $40$K, structured memory access is slower by $0.9$s. At $115$K,
\sys{} takes $3.4$s against $5.8$s, and at $1.5$M it is
$13.6\times$ faster. Structured access has a fixed cost at short histories;
reusable facts and bounded evidence processing avoid placing the entire
source history in each query as that history grows.

Full-Context's single memory-reading result call processes a longer prompt;
\sys{}'s $1.9$ calls use selected facts and unresolved evidence blocks.
The hierarchy controls context growth, while the executor controls
repeated semantic work.

Accuracy advantages over Full-Context remain $17.9$, $21.3$, and
$16.7$ points across the three variants.
The pipelines retain explicit coverage and binding checks while reusing
available facts; new evidence is acquired only when it is required.

\subsection{Case Study}
\label{subsec:exp9}

Table~\ref{tbl:evolution-case} traces three queries over Alice's
illustrative data from Examples~1--3, including the persistent updates.

\stitle{Stored content changes subsequent decisions.}
After Turn~1, $\Ff$ supplies verified settings and organizers; $\Uu$
distinguishes six returned facts from five credited by the use report. Turn~2 acquires
durations and records the acquisition outcome and cost. Repeating that
comparison can reuse its complete inputs with $K(q)=0$ additional calls.
The recorded action conditions also inform the cost of later acquisitions.

\stitle{Experience changes decisions at fixed content.}
With Turn~1's fact values fixed, the returned/used distinction still
changes access. The unit priors in Section~\ref{subsec:new-experience} assign
matched-contact use propensities $2/3$ to Ava/Cara links and $1/3$ to Ben's.
Pottery's indoor setting retains credit for exclusion. Source checks
determine eligibility; the learned values order equally relevant facts.
Recorded outcomes inform the predicted benefit and cost of later
missing-duration work; valid cached durations remove that work.

\stitle{A changed population changes the remaining work.}
Turn~3 adds one outing to the March scope. The three earlier setting
judgments remain reusable, while the new outing's actual setting requires
verification. The follow-up establishes that it occurred indoors, giving
$1+0+1+0=2$; without that evidence, the result remains $[2,3]$.
The cache removes completed checks; second-order experience guides the
new setting acquisition. Population coverage still determines whether
the count can be exact.
